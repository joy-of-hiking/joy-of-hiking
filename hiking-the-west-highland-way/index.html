Hiking the West Highland Way

October 7, 2025October 3, 2025 by hayaldeniz

After hiking on several trails on my own in the past years, I hiked along the West Highland Way, a 150 km long distance trail in Scotland, with a good friend of mine, Wiebke. We passed through magical forests, slept in mice-full mountain bothies, swam in the beautiful Loch Lomond, and got soaked by the Scottish rain. I discovered not only how beautiful the nature in Scotland is but also how wonderful it is to hike in the company of an old friend. This is the story of our hike in late August 2025 from Milngavie to Tyndrum.

Why West Highland Way?

For a long time, I didn’t consider hiking in Scotland, even though I had heard about the beautiful nature there, because of the complexity of obtaining a tourist visa for me. So, I explored different trails in other parts of Europe. However, after finally getting German citizenship earlier this year, my friend Wiebke reminded me of the idea of hiking in Scotland. About 5 months before our hike, I called Wiebke to ask her about her new rooster. She has lovely chickens in her backyard, and has lately gotten a new rooster. And then, our one-hour call ended with an initial plan to hike the West Highland Way together. Sometimes you don’t need long hours of research to decide on your next hike, it can be as easy and spontaneous as an hour call, thanks to a new rooster.

Prologue – Glasgow

Before starting our hike, we stayed the night before in Glasgow to get some supplies. On Buchanan Street we found a Tiso to get a gas canister and smidges, a repellent spray you want to have with you against the midges. We already had midges hats, but after some research on how annoying these littles flies can get, we decided to be more prepared for them then less. You can also get some smidge later on on your hike in several campsites, we saw some in Drymen Camping and later in Tyndrum as well. If you haven’t heard about midges yet, then check the midges forecast before on https://www.smidgeup.com/midge-forecast/. After getting our supplies, we enjoyed listening to music by buskers on Buchanan street and the super tasty food at the Stereo Bar, a big recommendation for a great meal to be prepared for your upcoming hike.


Day 1 – Milngavie to Drymen Camping, 19 km 

In the morning, I woke up very early, excited and curious about our hike in the Scottish Highlands. It would be Wiebke’s first long-distance hike ever, and my first hike in Scotland. We took the train from the Central Station and learned through a friendly encounter with the ticket office how to pronounce Milngavie, or at least we thought so (it’s really not that trivial!). After a half-hour train ride, we arrived in the town of Milngavie. Here, before starting your hike, you can get last-minute supplies from a pharmacy and a store with gas canisters and other hiking items just next to the marking of the trail’s start.

Right after starting the trail, we were already on nature paths, not something I usually experienced on long-distance trails. We saw many friendly people walking their dogs, squirrels and after some kilometers the first honesty box at the Cafe “Turnip the Beet”. There are several honesty boxes along the way where you can get delicious snacks, water or band aids.

For lunch, we found an easy spot on the way and  cooked noodles that we bought the day before in Glasgow and boy, they were so delicious. From that moment on, we gave ourselves the new hiking nickname “speedy noodles” as the noodle’s brand.

We ended our hike after around 19 km. in Drymen Camping. After we pitched our tent between some trees, we were welcomed by a sneaky chicken, with whom Wiebke quickly bonded. We cooked our dinner, some couscous mixed with lentils, and after chatting with fellow hikers, we went to bed in our tent. 

Day 2 – Drymen Camping to Sallochy, 25 km

After a nice breakfast with overnight oats that Wiebke had specially prepared for our trip, we were ready to start our second day. Before continuing on the WHW, we made a short trip to the town of Drymen to pick up some food supplies. Although the supermarket was much smaller than we had hoped, we were still able to pack enough for the upcoming days. If you decide to spend your first night in Drymen, you’ll also find Scotland’s oldest pub there. After walking the extra kilometer to get back on the WHW, we were once again surrounded by nature, with sheep all around us.

Shortly after midday, we reached Conic Hill, having left behind the long but beautiful ascent. From the top, we had a stunning view of Loch Lomond and its islands. For my already tired knees, the descent to Balmaha wasn’t easy — and the rain only made it more slippery.

It rained a lot during this trip, and this day was no exception. Thankfully, in Balmaha, we were able to dry off a bit at Café Oak Tree Inn, where we had a warm lunch — and Wiebke tried haggis for the first time!


The second day as described in https://www.westhighlandway.org officially ends in Rowerdannan after 24 kms. However, we decided to pitch our tent at the Sallochy campsite, 5 km. before Rowerdannan, a beautiful campsite just next to the Loch Lomond with basic facilities. Although wild camping is generally permitted along the West Highland Way, the section from Drymen till Rowerdannan is part of the camping management zone and camping is only permitted in campsites or designated permit areas. We hadn’t applied for a permit beforehand, but a fellow hiker told us it’s not too difficult to get one. For us, camping next to Loch Lomond was the perfect end to our second day. You can book the campsite in advance, and even pay a little extra for firewood — which will be waiting at your pitch, ready to warm you up in the evening.

Day 3 – Sallochy Campsite to Rowchoish Bothy, 11 km

After a long night of heavy rain and the panic of thinking I’d lost my phone, we decided to slow down a bit in the morning and enjoy our surroundings. Since our tent was pitched right next to Loch Lomond and everyone else still seemed to be sleeping, we went into the lake and had a nice morning swim. I believe that it’s so important to make space for these moments on long distance hikes to step out of survival mode and truly enjoy the places.

Our boots were still soaked from the day before, so we had to get creative to keep hiking in them. Luckily, we had some spare plastic bags with us and turned them into plastic hiking socks, an extra layer between our boots and the (hopefully) dry socks we were wearing. It actually worked: the socks stayed dry much longer, and our feet were much warmer than they would have been otherwise. The smell inside the boots was awful, but that’s nothing new. 🙂

The official third day of the West Highland Way ends in Inverarnan after a 22 km. stretch from Rowerdennan. This section is quite challenging and can get very slippery, especially north of Inversnaid you need constant attention and surefootedness. We decided to take two days instead of one for this part of the trail to be able to enjoy nature, stay safe and also because we were curious about the two mountain bothies along the way where hikers could stay in for the night to get a good night’s sleep while staying dry. For us this was the best decision and I would recommend going the whole stretch in one day to only experienced hikers who are short in time. 

After leaving Rowerdennan, we chose the lower route which is said to be technically more difficult but also more beautiful than the upper path. We passed through beautiful bridges along a challenging but fun path with constantly going up and down stairs and rocks, right next to Loch Lomond.

When we finally reached the flat, magical forest where the Rowchoish Bothy was waiting for us, we were more than happy. The bothy was used to be the home of a family in the 18th century and is now used as a simple shelter by hikers, maintained by volunteers. We shared the bothy with three fellow hikers and after putting our sleeping mats and sleeping bags on the floor, we were ready to rest.

Before settling in for the night, make sure to pack all your food into a bag and hang it somewhere mice can’t reach, this way, you’ll protect your supplies. Although I did find a bit of mouse poop on my backpack the next morning… probably from some very angry mice who couldn’t find anything to eat.

Day 4 – Rowchoish Bothy to Doune Byre, 11 km 

We had decided to make a short day to rest our knees and stay in the upcoming bothy which was only 11 km away. After saying goodbye to the Rowchoish Bothy and a rather easy walk, we arrived at the Inversnaid Hotel, just before noon. As with everyone else we’d met on our trip through Scotland, the people there were very kind and talkative. We got some warm lunch in an area designated for hikers where you have to leave your hiking boots before entering (which interestingly didn’t smell bad at all with all the naked hiker feet).

The stretch after Inversnaid was the hardest for us throughout the entire route up to Tyndrum. The terrain is quite challenging and although it was only 5 km, the rain and the slippery rocks made walking definitely harder. Just before we reached our destination, a fellow hiker had to be rescued by helicopter after falling on the slippery trail and injuring their head. As we found out later, accidents along this stretch happen often, so my advice would be to take your time for this part of the trail.

In the evening, we came across midges so heavily for the first time that we needed to put our midge hats on to go to the toilet. In the night, we shared the bothy with another fellow hiker, and as in the night before, secured our food from the mice. However, Wiebke still found in the next morning that some parts of her backpack were nibbled by mice. We were not able to start a fire in the fireplace of the bothy, so instead we put our wet socks and t-shirts inside our sleeping bags to use the natural heat of our bodies to hopefully have some dry socks in the morning. 

Day 5 – Doune Byre to Tyndrum, 24 km

After a very good night’s sleep under the better-insulated roof of Doune Byre, we woke up feeling energized and ready for a long day. Outside, the rain had stopped, and our clothes had dried after being kept in the sleeping bag the entire night.

After a few kilometers, we arrived at Beinglas Campsite, a favorite pitch spot for many hikers along the WHW. We got some snacks from its little shop and continued on our way, leaving Loch Lomond behind.

What started as a beautiful day with the rain having stopped, changed after some hills that we passed through and left us with heavy rain. Even our plastic socks couldn’t help anymore after holding up for the past few days. We passed through hills, beautiful forests and heathlands, hoping to find a warm bed in Tyndrum. Our destination in mind was the Tyndrum By The Way Hostel, as they seemed to have a great drying room to dry all the wet clothes we had been carrying with us the past couple of days. After arriving we got a warm hut for ourselves which was very necessary.

And The End…

Until now, we had walked 90 kms through rain, slippery and challenging terrain. I was impressed by how well Wiebke was coming along, although it was her first long-distance hike. I remembered how my first long distance hike went about 8 years ago, and I admired her strength and joy.

A look at the weather forecast showed us that the rain would only get worse the upcoming days with little chance to stay under a roof due to no affordable hotels and no bothies on the way. We had two options: either we could continue the way with lots of heavy rainfall, wind and wet clothing, or we could decide to leave the way here and get back to Edinburgh to spend some days before heading back to Germany. I don’t know if it’s me getting older, but there were times when I would take the bare minimum just to continue, just to finish what I had planned for. This time however, I saw only my ego as only reason to continue. I would rather come back and enjoy the Highlands in better conditions and spending some days exploring Edinburgh didn’t sound bad at all.

The next morning, we were warm, well-rested and ready to enjoy the rest of our holiday. We met another fellow hiker who had a rough night in her tent and decided to head to Edinburgh as well. Soon after, on our way to the train station, the sun came out for real. Although it lasted about 10 seconds, we could snap a photo of our happiness.


So, another adventure was over and while we were heading to Edinburgh on the train, I could look back and remember the last wonderful days spent on the West Highland Way. We decided to come back to Scotland, whether it will be West Highland Way or another trail (as I found later on https://www.walkhighlands.co.uk/long-distance-routes.shtml) we don’t know yet, but we’ll let you know 🙂
